# Process

I used ChatGPT during the development of this essay.

## How I used AI

I used it mainly for:

1. brainstorming possible arguments
2. testing different essay structures
3. challenging my initial assumptions
4. editing sentences for clarity
5. helping me connect the argument to my own creative practice

I did not use AI to generate the final essay directly. I used its suggestions as a way to develop and question my own argument, then rewrote and selected the ideas that felt relevant to my experience.

## Something I kept

One suggestion I kept was the distinction between “describing” an outcome and “specifying” a system.

I found this useful because it connected directly to my experience of making my short film *Source*. I did not need programming to make the film. I could describe a visual direction to an AI tool, generate different images, and then select and edit the results.

This made me realise that describing an image and defining how a creative system behaves are different things. For example, an interactive version of *Source* would require me to define what makes the environment change, how the audience's movement affects it, and how different elements respond to each other.

This gave me a more specific reason for learning programming. I became interested in the gap between “make this” and “make this happen under these conditions.”

## Something I rejected

ChatGPT initially suggested that AI cannot replace human creativity because humans have emotions and intentions.

I rejected this argument because it felt generic and too easy. It also did not answer the actual question of why I personally should learn programming.

The rejection helped me realise that I did not need to argue that humans are simply more creative than AI. Instead, my argument should focus on the difference between generating an output and understanding or defining the system behind it.

This led me to focus on description, specification, behaviour and interaction, using my own experience with *Source* as the main example.
